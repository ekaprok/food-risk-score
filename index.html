How Food Security Risk Score is calculated

Where the Data Comes From

All metrics are built from four public FAOSTAT (Food and Agriculture Organization) datasets:

  • Production: Tracks domestic harvest quantities (P).
  • National Trade: Tracks total national imports (I) and exports (E).
  • Bilateral Trade Matrix: Tracks shipment volumes between specific trading partners, used to measure supplier concentration.
  • Food Balances: Tracks daily per-capita calorie intake to assess how central a crop is to the national diet.

Scores cover 2020–2024. Every figure below is per country, per crop, per year.

The risk score

  1. Supply balance

    Supply = P + I − E

    Production plus imports less exports, in tonnes.

  2. Weights

    W_internal = P / (P + I)
    W_external = I / (P + I)

    Weights are calculated as a share of total inflows (production + imports).

  3. Internal risk — how unsteady the harvest is

    Risk_internal = std(P) / mean(P)

    The coefficient of variation of production over the trailing five years, including the current one. 0 means the harvest never moves; the larger it gets, the more production swings year to year.

  4. External risk — how concentrated the suppliers are

    Risk_external = ∑ s_i²

    A Herfindahl–Hirschman index, where s_i is supplier i's share of the flows the trade matrix records that year. It is 1/n when n suppliers ship equal amounts, and 1.0 when one supplier ships everything — so losing it is high risk.

  5. Criticality — how much the population relies on the commodity

    Criticality = Kcal_commodity / Kcal_total

  6. Vulnerability

    V = W_internal × Risk_internal + W_external × Risk_external

    The two risks, each weighted by how much of the inflow it governs.

  7. The headline score

    Food risk = V × Criticality

    Vulnerability scaled by how much the diet depends on the crop.

The supplier shock simulation

The simulation asks one question: what if the single biggest supplier stopped shipping entirely?

  1. The biggest supplier's share

    s_max = Volume_max / Volume_tracked

    The largest supplier's volume over all volume the trade matrix records for that year.

  2. Shock exposure

    Shock_exposure = W_external × s_max × Shock_magnitude

    The exact share of supply lost during a disruption. Shock_magnitude defaults to 1.0 to represent a total supplier collapse, but can be lowered to simulate partial supply chain cuts.

  3. Simulated vulnerability

    V_sim = V + (1 − V) × Shock_exposure

  4. The simulated score

    Food risk (simulated) = V_sim × Criticality

    Scaled by the same criticality, so it can be read directly against the score on the left.

Missing data

When years are missing from the source files, the National Trade and Bilateral Trade Matrix datasets default to zero. The Food Balances dataset carries the previous year's data forward.

Countries missing calorie data

The Food Balances dataset has no data for three of the countries scored here. We assign each a fixed Criticality proxy, borrowed from a comparable country:

  • Sudan — 0.32, from Djibouti in 2023
